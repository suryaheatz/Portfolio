Haptix thumbwheel demonstrator

I was the UI and UX designer on a demonstrator that ZF built to pitch a new thumbwheel technology for operating a car interface. I worked out the flows and interactions in workshops, linked what the hardware can do to what the screen does, designed the screens, prototyped them and handed them to the developers. I also worked on the demonstrator’s concept styling, the thumbwheel design and its CMF (colour, material and finish).

Brief

The brief

ZF pitched this demonstrator to promote a new thumbwheel technology for operating a vehicle interface. A demonstrator is a working model built to show a technology. The interface (HMI, short for human-machine interface) is the screen and controls the driver uses. The customer was Mercedes-Benz (Daimler).

The engineering team gave me the specification and the capabilities of the hardware. The specification said what the thumbwheel can do. It did not say what the screen should show or how a person should operate it. I had to design the interaction models and the UI that make use of those capabilities.

Before I designed anything, I studied Mercedes-Benz interfaces. I visited showrooms and used the interfaces in the cars.

The hardware

What the thumbwheel can do

The thumbwheel has three actions. You can turn it, push it, and touch it. The touch is sensed along the wheel’s axis, so the system knows which part of the wheel your thumb is on. I call these parts the left, centre and right segments.

The damper behind the wheel is switched off when nothing touches the wheel, because it would overheat otherwise. Touching a segment switches it on. The damper changes how hard the wheel is to turn.

The specification lists the feels the damper can produce: ripple at low, medium and high strength, constant resistance with or without rising force, constant resistance with ripples, and a barrier in each direction.

The sensors, damper, controller and electronics were specified by the ZF engineering team.

Left segment

Turns the value in the left column.

Centre segment

Moves through the centre list. Push confirms.

Right segment

Turns the value in the right column.

03 · Hardware to UI

Linking the hardware to the screen

This is the interaction model. Each row is one wheel action and what the screen does in response.

Wheel actionScreen response
Touch a segmentThe column above that segment lights up. The damper switches on.
Turn the left or right segmentThe value in that column changes: temperature, seat pose, volume, song, an options list or an artist list.
Turn the centre segmentThe highlighted row in the centre list moves, for example from Climate to Seat Modes.
Push the left or right segmentThe tab on that side opens.
Push the centre segmentThe highlighted row is confirmed and flashes.
Turn past the end of a rangeThe wheel refuses to turn (a “barrier”). The screen stays on the last value.

In the screens, the left column is the Driver and the right column is the Co-Driver.

The recreation

How the 2026 recreation works

The 2020 prototype was in Figma. In 2026 I rebuilt the interaction model as the browser prototype at the top of this page, so that visitors can operate the wheel themselves. It is a recreation made for this portfolio. It was not part of the 2020 project.

On the wheelWith a mouseOn a touch screen
TouchMove the pointer onto a segmentTouch and hold a segment
TurnHold and drag up or down on a segmentTouch and drag up or down
PushClickTap

The mouse wheel and the arrow keys also turn the wheel. A browser cannot make the damper’s feel, so a force gauge shows the effort you apply. The gauge pins and turns red at a barrier.

A guided sequence of 16 steps walks through all screens and all three tabs. On touch screens it has 15 steps, because the first step (touching without pushing) cannot be done there with a tap. Each step has a title that states the result, for example “Driver temp 16° → 20°C”. It lists what to do and what the wheel should feel like. If you are on the wrong screen for a step, the card shows the move that gets you there instead of an error.

On desktop, a Gentle, Standard and Firm setting changes how far you must drag for each notch.

On a phone, the recreation fits one screen without page scroll. The top shows the display, cropped to the tabs and menus. The middle shows the current step. The bottom shows the wheel and the force gauge. A bar of icon buttons stays at the bottom: back, skip, sound, restart and the full step list.

  • A short tap pushes.
  • Touch and drag turns.
  • A long press (about 0.4 seconds) wakes the segment and holds it.
  • Phones that support vibration vibrate on each notch and each barrier.

These three things were decided while building the recreation. They were not part of the 2020 design.

  • Looping tabs. A push on the left segment opens the left tab, and a push on the right segment opens the right tab. The label under each segment says where its push goes.
  • Comfort tab content. Ergonomics (Seat Modes) and Climate (Temperature), in two rows with end stops.
  • A feel for each menu, as a starting value for tuning on the hardware. The centre category list and the options list ripple at medium strength. Temperature and volume use constant resistance that rises. Seat poses ripple at high strength. The song, album and artist lists ripple at low strength. The Sound and Source rows are locked with a barrier.
  • Turning with a mouse is “hold and drag”. Turning on a touch screen is “touch and drag”. Click or tap is “push”. The words “scroll” and “roll” are not used.
  • Step titles state the result, so a viewer can check each step without reading the instructions.
  • A wrong turn shows the move that leads to the right menu, not an error message.
  • On a touch screen, a tap is a push, so a tap cannot wake a segment without pushing it. The recreation skips the wake step there, and a long press wakes a segment.
  • On small screens the step details start collapsed and open on tap, to save vertical space.

Limits

Limits and next steps

  • The recreation covers the screens that were designed in 2020. Sound and Source are not designed, so their rows are locked.
  • The value ranges in the recreation (14 to 28 °C, volume 0 to 30, 12 songs, 9 list items) and the Titles, Playlists and Recently Played lists are extended from the patterns in the screens.
  • A browser cannot reproduce the feel of the wheel. The force gauge stands in for it.
  • Text contrast on the display was set by eye and has not been measured.
